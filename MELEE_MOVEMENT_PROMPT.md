# Implementation prompt for Thrash Fighters

You are working in the existing Thrash Fighters repository. Implement a movement, input, and combat overhaul that makes the game feel close to NTSC Super Smash Bros. Melee 1.02 in a competitive one-on-one match. The immediate goals are faster and more responsive movement, distinct Fox-style and Falco-style physics and movesets, reliable tilts, and a dedicated short-hop bind. Deliver a playable Windows build and evidence of how closely it matches the reference.

Keep the Godot 4.7.2 .NET and C# architecture. The standalone simulation owns authoritative gameplay at 60 ticks per second. Godot captures input, displays the simulation, and plays sound. Keep controls, keyboard and gamepad profiles, solo and local versus, training, stock rules, replay and frame step, pause, and the winner podium working through their existing entry points.

Keep simulation time in ticks. Compare movement on controlled flat ground, a one-way platform, and an edge using matching starting positions and controller sequences. Save the pre-change and post-change results.

## Make waveshine and aerial pressure playable

Make this Kitsune sequence a required playtest in the Windows build. **Wavedash in → shine → jump-cancel into short-hop drill → L-cancel → shine → jump-cancel into another wavedash → read the next opening.** A waveshine is the shine, jump, and downward diagonal air dodge at the end of that sequence. Use the actual player inputs and legal action transitions for every link. In Training, provide a reproducible starting distance, facing, opponent, damage, and defensive settings for one situation where the route works.

1. The wavedash-in slide should reach shine range with actionable frames to spare when the player starts from the tested spacing. Start shine on the first legal grounded tick after landing recovery. Check both facing directions and multiple dodge angles so the slide changes the opening.
2. Verify shine’s jump-cancel window and keep it separate from the move’s hitbox and reflector windows. After a legal jump cancel, run the usual jump squat and short-hop takeoff. Start down-air on a legal airborne tick. Verify the drill’s separate hit pulses, rehit timing, hitlag, and the defender’s ability to shift out of the drill with SDI.
3. The player times a fresh shield input before an eligible drill landing. Compare that landing with a missed L-cancel and an autocancel. Record how many ticks elapse before the next shine can start, and verify that the follow-up depends on the actual landing recovery. [Melee aerial landing reference](https://github.com/doldecomp/melee/blob/master/src/melee/ft/kinds/ftCommon/ftCo_LandingAir.c)
4. Jump-cancel the next shine, use a downward diagonal air dodge, and let the swept floor contact and traction produce the wavedash. Verify that the player can choose the slide direction and then chase, grab, or reset spacing according to the defender’s position. [Melee air-dodge reference](https://github.com/doldecomp/melee/blob/master/src/melee/ft/kinds/ftCommon/ftCo_EscapeAir.c)

Record the working route and a near miss in Training. Check each timing window a tick early and late. Test grounded hit, airborne hit, shield, and whiff separately. Vary defender weight, percent, SDI during drill, DI on launch, tech options on knockdown, and distance to the ledge. Identify which links are true combos, which are shield pressure, and which require a read or a chase. Confirm that defenders can escape or punish when their state and the actual frame advantage allow it.
